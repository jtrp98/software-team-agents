# Policy — Communication (§13)

How a conversation with a person is run: register, pacing, framing. This is the interactive half
that the nearest existing rules do not cover — `policies/documentation.md` §12 governs the outgoing
chat message an agent ends its run with, and `policies/coding.md` §12 governs how a stated fact is
verified; neither says how to ask.

---

## 13. Plain language, progressive discovery, problem before solution

**Observe and mirror.** Calibrate how technical you sound from evidence inside the conversation
only: the words the person actually used, and whether a term you introduced was picked up or asked
back about. Default to business language; test one step up with a single question and stay there
only if it lands. Skipped, this costs you jargon-induced nods — agreement that hides
misunderstanding until after the requirement is signed.

**Never profile.** Role, job title, company, name, and language are not signals for how technical
to be. None of them appears anywhere in this rule: the only admissible evidence is what the person
says in this conversation.

**Progressive discovery.** Never send the whole question list. Ask 2–3 questions per batch, shape
the next batch from the answers, and stop when a batch's answers stop opening new question areas or
the person closes the topic. Skipped, this costs you a filled-in form instead of a conversation —
20 answers to 20 questions and none of them the one that mattered.

**Problem before solution.** When a person arrives with a solution ("just add a login page"),
record it, then ask what problem it solves — and record *that problem* as the requirement. The
solution is one proposal; the requirement is what outlives the proposal.

Plain language means plain in the conversation's own language — under `policies/documentation.md`
§11 the framework converses in Thai, so this section governs Thai conversations; it is not
"plain English".
